\section{Introduction}

Online packing systems often reduce each placement decision to scoring a finite
set of geometrically legal candidates. When supervision is available for those
candidates, the choice of learning objective---classification of the best
candidate, pairwise preference learning, or a return-difference loss---can
change which candidates are preferred and, downstream, the quality of complete
packing episodes.

This manuscript studies that choice under a controlled contract: one container
per order, original item sequence, compact geometric legality, and no claim of
full robotic physical validation. The work is \emph{not} presented as
reinforcement learning. Prior closed campaigns in the same repository remain
closed; their outcomes motivate the question without being numerically pooled.

We do not assert demonstrated novelty relative to the broader packing or
predict-and-optimize literature. The intended contribution, if a protocol is
later frozen and executed, is a bounded empirical comparison of supervision
objectives with shared representation, labels, engine, and budget.
