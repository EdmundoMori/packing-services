\section{Introduction}

In online three-dimensional packing, each arriving item must be assigned a
feasible placement before the remainder of the order is observed in full.
Practical engines expose a finite set of geometrically legal candidates and score
them with a heuristic or a learned scorer
\citep{crainic2008extreme,zhao2022pct}. When candidate-level supervision is
available, the \emph{learning objective} can change which placements are preferred
and, through sequential packing, complete-episode geometric utilisation.

This manuscript reports a \emph{controlled empirical comparison}, not a
demonstrated algorithmic improvement. The scientific question is:
\begin{quote}
Under a fixed geometric contract, a shared candidate encoder, a shared local
support map $S$, shared labels and a shared optimisation budget, how do
classification, pairwise preference and return-difference supervision affect
complete-episode geometric utilisation?
\end{quote}
The primary contrast compares preference learning with classification after paired
seeds. A return-difference arm is secondary \citep{mandi2022decision}.
GreedyBestFit on the full legal list is the mandatory deployment reference.
PCT policies are related work only \citep{zhao2022pct}.

Orders come from the BED-BPP collection \citep{kagerer2023bedbpp}. Evaluation uses
this repository's compact geometric contract; official robotic stability simulation
is not used. The field \codepath{physical_stability_verified} remains null throughout.

The frozen campaign completed labelling, paired training and a full development
episode evaluation. A preregistered development gate did not pass; the held-out
test split was not opened. The contribution is therefore a bounded, auditable
negative result under the stated configuration rather than evidence of superiority
of any objective.
