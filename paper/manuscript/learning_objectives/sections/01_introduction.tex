\section{Introduction}

In online three-dimensional packing, each arriving item must be assigned a
feasible placement before the remainder of the order is observed in full.
Practical engines therefore expose a finite set of geometrically legal
candidates and score them with a heuristic or a learned scorer
\citep{crainic2008extreme,zhao2022pct}. When candidate-level supervision is
available, the \emph{learning objective}---how mismatches between scores and
labels are penalised---can change which placements are preferred and, through
the sequential packing process, the utilisation of complete episodes.

The scientific question of this manuscript is therefore:
\begin{quote}
Under a fixed geometric contract, a shared candidate encoder, a shared local
support map $S$, shared labels and a shared optimisation budget, how do
classification, pairwise preference and return-difference supervision affect
complete-episode geometric utilisation?
\end{quote}
The primary contrast compares preference learning with classification after
paired seeds. A Mandi-inspired return-difference loss is retained as a
secondary arm \citep{mandi2022decision}. GreedyBestFit remains a mandatory
practical reference; packing-configuration-tree (PCT) policies are discussed
only as related work and are not experimental baselines here
\citep{zhao2022pct}.

Orders are drawn from the BED-BPP instance collection
\citep{kagerer2023bedbpp}. The evaluation metric and constraints follow a
compact geometric contract defined in this repository, not the official robotic
BED-BPP evaluation with rigid-body stability simulation. Physical stability is
recorded as null and is not claimed.

The intended contribution is a bounded empirical comparison of supervision
objectives for local packing actions with continuation labels and episode-level
readouts. Demonstrated superiority, statistical confirmation and publication
readiness remain contingent on executing the frozen protocol; they are not
asserted in this draft.
