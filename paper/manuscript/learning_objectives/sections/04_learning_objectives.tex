\section{Learning objectives}

All arms share architecture, labelled states constructed on $S_t$, normalisation
fitted on train only, initialisation, checkpoint rule and the support map $S$.
They differ only in the state-wise loss. Scores are larger-is-better. Let
$s\in\mathbb{R}^{|S_t|}$ denote scores and $Q\in\mathbb{R}^{|S_t|}$ the constant
continuation labels. Write $\varepsilon=10^{-9}$. Losses below are implemented as
autograd-compatible tensor maps that match the frozen scalar reference formulae.

\paragraph{Classification.}
Let
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{B}(t)=\bigl\{a\in S_t:\ |Q_a-\max_{b\in S_t}Q_b|\le\varepsilon\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The target places mass $1/|\mathcal{B}(t)|$ on each element of $\mathcal{B}(t)$
(and zero elsewhere). With $p=\mathrm{softmax}(s)$ and no temperature,
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{cls}}(t)=-\sum_{a\in S_t} \mathbf{1}[a\in\mathcal{B}(t)]\,\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}(t)|}\,\log p_a.
\end{equation}
If every return ties, $\mathcal{B}(t)=S_t$ and the target is uniform. A singleton
$S_t$ yields loss zero under a one-point softmax.

\paragraph{Existing preferences.}
For unordered pairs $\{i,j\}$ with $i<j$, let $\Delta Q_{ij}=Q_i-Q_j$. Strict pairs
($|\Delta Q_{ij}|>\varepsilon$) contribute
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{ij}=\mathrm{softplus}\bigl(-(s_{\mathrm{high}}-s_{\mathrm{low}})\bigr),
\end{equation}
oriented toward the higher return, with weight $w_{ij}=|\Delta Q_{ij}|$ renormalised
to sum to one over strict pairs in the state. Tied pairs contribute
$(s_i-s_j)^2$. With tie coefficient $1$,
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{pref}}(t)
  =
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathrm{strict}} \tilde w_{ij}\,\ell_{ij}
  +
  \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{ties}\neq\emptyset]\,
  \frac{1}{|\mathrm{ties}|}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathrm{ties}}(s_i-s_j)^2.
\end{equation}
If there are no strict pairs (including $|S_t|=1$), the pairwise term is zero. This
is not Mandi et al.'s pairwise-difference regression.

\paragraph{Return-difference adaptation.}
Following Mandi et al.'s Section~4.3, Equation~(13)
\citep{mandi2022decision}, define ordered pairs
$\mathrm{OP}=\{(p,q):Q_p>Q_q+\varepsilon\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{rd}}(t)
  =
  \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathrm{OP}|}
  \sum_{(p,q)\in\mathrm{OP}}
  \bigl((s_p-s_q)-(Q_p-Q_q)\bigr)^2,
  & \mathrm{OP}\neq\emptyset,\\[1em]
  0, & \mathrm{OP}=\emptyset.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The empty-pair case remains connected to the score graph with zero gradient.
Relative to Mandi, we substitute local continuation returns for combinatorial
solution values and average over all strict ordered pairs inside $S_t$.

\paragraph{State reduction and calibration.}
Training minimises the arithmetic mean of state losses with equal weight per
state. The three losses are not rescaled to a common numeric range; a shared
Adam schedule enables comparison under a fixed configuration, not a claim that
the objectives are equally calibrated or equally easy to optimise.
