\title{Learning Objectives for Candidate Selection in Online 3D Packing:\\
A Controlled Empirical Study}
\author{Provisional draft compiled from the packing-services research archive}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We report a controlled comparison of three candidate-level learning objectives
(classification, pairwise preferences, and return-difference supervision) for
online three-dimensional packing under a fixed geometric contract, a shared
17-feature present encoder, and a shared deployment support map $S$ that scores at most
four alternatives per decision. Nine models were trained with forty full-batch
Adam steps each on forty-eight BED-BPP train orders (paired seeds) and evaluated
on twenty-four development orders
(twenty-four GreedyBestFit episodes on the full legal list plus two hundred
sixteen support-constrained actors). After averaging seeds within each order and
then averaging orders with equal weight, mean episode geometric utilisation margin
preferences$-$classification was $-0.00808$; both learned arms were below
GreedyBestFit (preferences$-$Greedy $\approx -0.0516$,
classification$-$Greedy $\approx -0.044$). A preregistered development gate did
not pass; the held-out test split was not opened. The study therefore documents
a bounded negative result under this configuration on development only, not
superiority of any objective, physical stability, or generalisation beyond
development.
\end{abstract}
