\section{Introduction}

Online three-dimensional packing for industrial order sequences must often act
under imperfect \emph{dimensional} information: realised item extents can
deviate from the nominal catalogue sizes used by a planner. Prior robotic
packing studies document a reliability--compactness trade-off under fixed
protective buffers and under deterministic clearance margins. This outline
concerns geometric \emph{protection management} only: synthetic dimensional
uncertainty on BED-BPP nominal orders, sequences, and own-target containers.
Position error, deformation, physical stability, robot trajectories, and
post-collision recovery are out of the initial scope.

The provisional question is whether a sequential policy can choose a
protective envelope online ($p=s=1$) and retain more \emph{nominal} placed
volume than fixed-margin baselines under a common geometric-failure risk
measure. BED-BPP contributes nominal catalogue data only. Reinforcement
learning is not assumed necessary: support-bounded uncertainty yields a
directly computable conservative baseline (and makes learning trivial under
full guarantee), while genuine stochastic sensitivity---still pending a
corrected protocol---must be compared first to analytical rules that share
the same public law. Necessity of RL is not established.
